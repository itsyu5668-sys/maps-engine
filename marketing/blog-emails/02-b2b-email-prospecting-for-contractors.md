# B2B Email Prospecting for Contractor Leads

Contractors are the cleanest B2B lead generation vertical I have worked in. They run real businesses, they have real websites, and most of them publish a real contact email. If you sell to contractors, your list building problem is mostly solved by Google Maps plus a website scraper.

Here is how I build contractor email lists that convert at 8 to 12 percent reply rates.

## Why contractors are an easy vertical

A roofing company in Dallas is not hiding. They have a Google Maps listing, a website, a phone number, and a contact page with an info@ address. They want leads themselves, so they understand why you would contact them. The friction is low.

Compare that to enterprise SaaS targets, who hide behind contact forms and gatekeepers. Contractors are open. You just have to find them and email the right address.

## The list build

Pick a trade and a city. Run a query like "plumbers in Phoenix with emails". You get back every plumber Google Maps knows about in Phoenix, plus a public email scraped from each plumber's own website.

The row you get:

- name
- address
- phone
- website
- rating
- email

Six fields, all real, all from public sources.

## Qualify before you email

Do not email the whole list. Filter first.

**Rating filter.** Drop anything under 4.0. Low rated contractors have operational problems. They are not buyers, they are survivors.

**Website filter.** If a business has no website, they are too small or too old school for most B2B offers. Skip them.

**Email filter.** Only keep leads that returned an email. A lead with just a phone is a cold call, not a cold email.

After filtering, you usually keep 60 to 70 percent of the raw list. That is your send list.

## The email itself

Contractors respond to direct, specific outreach. Do not send a template. Reference their city, their trade, and something specific about their business.

A bad email: "Hi, I help businesses like yours grow online."

A good email: "Saw Priority Roofing is rated 5.0 across 200 reviews on Maps. Most roofers I talk to in Dallas are booked out 6 weeks but losing leads after hours. I built something that fixes that. Worth a 10 minute call Thursday?"

The second email uses the Maps data you already have. That is the whole point of scraping Maps instead of buying a list: you get the context that makes the email specific.

## Cost per lead

If you pay $2 per 1,000 results and roughly half return an email, your cost per email lead is about $4. For a contractor close rate of 5 percent on a $3,000/month retainer, the math works even if you are terrible at cold email.

## What to avoid

Do not buy a contractor email list from a data broker. Those lists are stale, the emails are guessed, and you will land in spam filters hard. Scrape the public email from the business's own site, or use a tool that does that for you. The deliverability difference is large.
