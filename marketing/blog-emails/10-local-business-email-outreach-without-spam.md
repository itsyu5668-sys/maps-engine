# Local Business Email Outreach That Does Not Get You Marked Spam

Getting marked as spam kills cold email programs. Once your domain reputation drops, even your good sends land in the junk folder. The fix is not a better template. The fix is better data, honest list building, and conservative sending.

Here is how I keep local business email outreach out of spam.

## Start with honest emails

The biggest spam trigger is sending to invalid addresses. Every bounce tells the receiving mail server you are not cleaning your list. After enough bounces, you get filtered.

The honest way to build a list is to scrape the public email from each business's own website. That email is real. It was published by the business. The bounce rate on scraped public emails is dramatically lower than on bought lists, because bought lists contain guessed pattern emails that do not exist.

Start honest, and you skip the main cause of spam filtering.

## Verify before you send

Even honest scraped emails can go dead. A business might have published info@company.com two years ago and switched providers. The email is on their site, but the inbox is gone.

Run a verification pass before you send. Tools like ZeroBounce or NeverBounce ping the mail server without sending a message and tell you if the inbox is live. Cost is low, usually a fraction of a cent per email. The savings from not bouncing are worth it.

A clean send list is a list of verified, deliverable emails. That is the only list you should send to.

## Warm your domain

Do not send 500 cold emails from a fresh domain on day one. Mail servers flag sudden volume from new senders.

Warm up slowly. Send 20 on day one, 40 on day two, 80 on day three. Ramp over two weeks to your target daily volume. Use a warmup tool if you have one, or just send to friendly inboxes first to build a sending history.

If you are using a new domain for outreach, warm it for at least two weeks before any cold send.

## Send volume conservatively

Even with a warmed domain and a clean list, do not send 1,000 cold emails per day from one inbox. Mail providers flag high volume cold sending.

I cap at 50 to 80 cold sends per inbox per day. If I need more volume, I add inboxes, not sends. Three inboxes at 50 per day is 150 per day, which is sustainable, versus 150 from one inbox, which is risky.

## Make the email easy to identify as not spam

Spam filters look at content too. A real cold email from a real person looks different from a spam blast.

- Use a real name in the from field, not "Marketing Team".
- Write a short, specific subject. "Quick question about your Dallas roofing leads" beats "Grow your business today".
- Keep the body under 100 words. Long emails with lots of links look like spam.
- Do not use spam trigger words: free, guarantee, act now, limited time.
- Include a real signature with your name, company, and a way to unsubscribe. The unsubscribe link is required by law in most places and it signals legitimacy to filters.

## The content test

Before you send, read your email out loud. If it sounds like a commercial, rewrite it. If it sounds like a note you would send to a specific person, you are in good shape.

The best cold emails sound like they were written for one recipient. That is because the data lets you be specific. You know the business name, the city, the rating, the trade. Use it.

A spammy email: "Dear Business Owner, we can help you grow your revenue by 300 percent. Click here for a free demo."

A real email: "Saw T Rock Roofing is at 4.9 in Dallas. Most roofers I talk to are booked out 6 weeks but losing leads after hours. I built a tool that captures those. Worth a 10 minute call Thursday?"

The second email will not get marked spam, because it is clearly a real person writing to a real business about a real problem.

## Monitor and adjust

Track your reply rate, bounce rate, and spam complaint rate per send. If bounces go above 2 percent, stop and reverify your list. If spam complaints go above 0.1 percent, stop and rewrite your copy.

Cold email is a system. Honest data, verification, warmup, conservative volume, and specific copy. Skip any step and the whole thing degrades.
