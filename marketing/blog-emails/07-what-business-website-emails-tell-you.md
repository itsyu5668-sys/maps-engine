# What Emails on Business Websites Actually Tell You

When you scrape a contact email from a business's own website, you get more than an address. The email itself is a signal. The local part, the domain, and whether it is a free address or a custom domain all tell you something about the business and how to approach them.

Here is what I look at when I read a scraped email.

## The local part

The part before the @ is the local part, and it tells you who is likely reading the inbox.

- **info@** is a general inbox. Usually the office manager or owner checks it. Good for first contact.
- **office@** is similar. Goes to the front desk or office manager.
- **hello@** is a modern marketing choice. Often a small business with a clean website.
- **contact@** is the safest bet. It is monitored because it is the public contact point.
- **service@** or **dispatch@** goes to operations. Skip for sales, use for vendor outreach.
- **sales@** means they are actively selling something. Good for B2B partnership outreach.
- **admin@** usually goes to the owner or IT. Can be hit or miss for replies.

When I scrape a list and see a mix, I prioritize info@, contact@, and office@ for cold outreach. They have the highest reply rate because someone actually reads them.

## The domain

The part after the @ tells you about the business's sophistication.

- **Custom domain** (info@arringtonroofing.com) means they invested in a real website. Usually a mid market business with budget. This is your best lead.
- **Gmail** (arringtonroofing@gmail.com) usually means a smaller or older business. Still a valid lead, but expect a smaller budget and a more cautious buyer.
- **Yahoo** (arringtonroofing@yahoo.com) is a red flag for an outdated operation. Often a one person shop. Low budget, high friction.
- **Hotmail or Outlook** similar to Yahoo. Older business owner, personal email on the public site.

I do not filter out free domain emails, but I segment them. Custom domain leads get the full pitch. Free domain leads get a shorter, simpler pitch because the owner is likely doing everything themselves.

## The free domain honesty note

I want to be clear about one thing. When I say I scrape the email from the business's own website, I mean the email they chose to publish. If a roofer put arringtonroofing@gmail.com on their contact page, that is the email I return. I do not swap it for a guessed info@arringtonroofing.com. The public email is the honest email.

Some businesses publish a free address because that is what they have. Respect that. Email the address they gave you.

## What the email does not tell you

The scraped email does not tell you:

- whether the inbox is actively monitored
- whether it is a catch all that accepts everything but reads nothing
- whether the person who reads it can make a buying decision

For those answers, you need a verification pass and a first send. The scrape gives you the address. The send tells you if it is live.

## Using the signal

When I build a list, I sort by email type before I send. Custom domain info@ leads go first, because they convert best. Free domain leads go second, with a simpler pitch. Service and dispatch emails go to a separate vendor outreach list.

That sort alone lifted my reply rate from 6 percent to 11 percent. The data was the same. The order was different.
